Intro · Workplace learning

New hires don’t ask. They wait.

The brief was open: find where an AI learning assistant creates value at work. Four weeks of interviews kept landing on the same moment, the first weeks in a new job, when people stay quiet so they don’t bother anyone.

The client’s hypothesis: if learning were embedded in the tasks people already do, guided by AI and tied to the company’s OKRs, it would happen implicitly and stay relevant.

Pillar 01Implicit learningLearning happens through real tasks and situations, guided by AI.
Pillar 02Continuous feedbackRegular, context specific feedback keeps people aware of their progress.
Pillar 03Competency measurementOngoing interactions and assessments show what actually improved.

The three pillars of the brief, paraphrased from the client’s kickoff deck. Under the hood shows how each became a bot behaviour.

Finding

Each sprint answered one risky question cheaply. Together they turned “a chatbot for onboarding” into three rules: give value first, give every answer an address, and hand off when it doesn’t know.

    Under the hood · Built on Microsoft Copilot

    Teams is the face. Copilot is the brain.

    People only ever see Teams. Behind it, Copilot reads the question, company files ground the answer, and the org chart finds a person when the files can’t. Tap a layer, or watch one question travel through.

    One question, four layersIllustrative trace · “How do I log my working hours?”
    From the brief to the bot: how new hires learn without a course
    Pillar 01Implicit learning
    →
    Learning in the task. Answers arrive in the chat at the moment of need, with the document and link, so every question teaches where things live.
    Pillar 02Continuous feedback
    →
    Nudges, not reports. A daily “here’s today”, a Friday recap, and a thumbs up or down on every answer that flags weak documents to their owner.
    Pillar 03Competency measurement
    →
    Progress the new hire sees first. Plan steps done and questions resolved, shown to them before their manager, and never a score on the person.
    What the agent stores, and what it never does+
    • Session memory of the conversation, plus profile, OKRs and goals, kept in our own services next to Copilot.
    • Answers only from files the person can already open in Microsoft 365, cited with document and date.
    • Expert replies are stored with the expert’s name and date, so the next answer has a source too.
    • It never scores or evaluates employees, asks before using anyone’s data, and keeps a working path when someone declines.

    Learnings

    What I’d keep, and do sooner.

    In conversational UX, the first turn is the product+
    v1 was technically impressive and lost people in its first message. A bot earns the right to ask questions by giving value first.
    Fake it before you build it+
    A person answering in Slack taught us what no prompt would have: answers need addresses, and people will ask a bot what they won’t ask a colleague.
    Design for the moment the AI doesn’t know+
    Trust came from the honest hand off, not the clever answer. Repair and fallback paths are part of the happy path.
    Write the problem statement with the client+
    Our Bull’s Eye was solid, but the client never co-signed the problem. Next time I’d get one sentence signed off by week 3 and revisit it in every review.
